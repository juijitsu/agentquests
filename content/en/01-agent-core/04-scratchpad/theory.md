# Theory · The notepad

On the previous level you decided what to keep before the work began: the terms
of the task are always needed, so they are always in the window. That works while
the important thing is known in advance.

Not everything is known in advance. A bridge weight limit turns up on the third
hop. It was not in the request, it never made it into the terms of the task, and
the decision at the end depends on exactly that.

## Two kinds of memory

**Static** — what you singled out while writing the code. The terms of the task,
the customer's parameters, the requirements. It lives in your variable and is
always put into the window.

**Dynamic** — what the agent discovered on the way and judged important. Neither
its content nor its quantity is known up front. That is the notepad.

The difference is not mechanical — both get put into the window. The difference
is **who decides** what goes there. In the first case you; in the second the
agent.

## Why a notepad and not the whole history

You could object: the limit was already in the history, in a tool result. Why
duplicate it?

Because the history gets compacted. Three hops later the result carrying the
limit leaves the window along with the rest of the tail — while the notepad
stays, because it is put in separately and whole.

**The notepad is what you took out of the stream and put in your pocket.** The
stream flows on; the pocket stays.

## What to write down

Not everything: the notepad takes slots in the window, and a bloated notepad
squeezes out the recent tail. What gets written down is what changes the
decision: limits, refusals, values found, promises made to the customer.

Reports on completed steps are not written down — they are visible in the tail
while they are current, and unneeded once they are stale.
